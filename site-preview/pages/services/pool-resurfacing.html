When Is It Time to Resurface Your Pool?

Pool plaster is a wear surface. In Florida's year-round swim climate, a standard plaster finish typically lasts 15 to 20 years before the surface breaks down — and the signs show up well before the shell itself is at risk. If your pool feels rough underfoot, snags swimsuits, or holds stains that no cleaning brings back, the finish is telling you it's done.

  • Rough, etched, or sandpaper-like plaster that scrapes feet and toes
  • Stains and discoloration that an acid wash can no longer remove
  • Visible spalling, flaking, or hollow spots where plaster has separated from the shell
  • Exposed gunite or dark patches showing through the finish
  • Water loss through cracks in the shell rather than plumbing or equipment
  • Plaster at or beyond the 15–20 year mark, even if it still "looks okay" from the deck
Not sure whether your pool needs a resurface or just an acid wash? We'll tell you honestly. If the finish has life left, an acid wash is the far smaller job — and we do both.

Finish Options: Quartz, Pebble & Polished Aggregate

Resurfacing is also the one chance every couple of decades to choose how your pool looks and feels. We install the three main finish families and walk you through samples before you commit:

  • Quartz finishes — plaster blended with crushed quartz for a harder, longer-lasting surface with rich color options and a smooth feel
  • Pebble finishes — exposed pebble aggregate with a natural, textured look and the longest service life of the three
  • Polished aggregate — pebble or glass bead aggregate polished smooth, combining aggregate durability with a silky underfoot feel

Each family trades differently on feel, appearance, and longevity. We help you match the finish to how your household actually uses the pool — bare feet and kids, or looks-first entertaining.

Our Resurfacing Process

  • Drain & assess — controlled drain of the pool, then a full inspection of the shell, returns, main drain, and fittings while everything is visible
  • Surface prep & chip-out — removal of failed plaster, bond coat preparation, and repair of any cracks or hollow spots before new material goes on
  • Finish application — your chosen quartz, pebble, or polished aggregate finish applied and troweled by crews who do this work daily
  • Fill & startup chemistry — a managed refill followed by the startup water-chemistry sequence the finish manufacturer requires, so the new surface cures correctly instead of scaling or staining in its first month

The Drain Is the Best Inspection You'll Ever Get

Here's the angle most resurfacing crews can't offer: while your pool is empty, everything normally hidden underwater is exposed — light niches, conduit, bonding connections, fittings, and the equipment that serves them. A plaster-only contractor has to look past all of it.

Perfect Catch holds both pool and electrical contractor licenses, so during your resurface the same team inspects your pool lights, niches, and bonding, and looks over your pump, filter, and plumbing connections. If a light fixture is at end of life or bonding needs correction, the drained pool is the cheapest possible moment to fix it — no dive work, no second drain, no second contractor.

  • Pool light and niche inspection while the fixture is dry and accessible
  • Bonding and grounding check on wet-niche fixtures and metal fittings
  • Equipment once-over — pump, filter, and plumbing connections — while the system is down anyway
  • One contractor, one schedule: finish work and electrical corrections in the same drain

Resurfacing is also the natural moment to think bigger — new waterline tile, lighting upgrades, or equipment modernization. If you're considering more than the finish, see our pool remodeling & renovation services.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does pool plaster last?

A standard plaster finish typically lasts 15 to 20 years in Florida conditions. Quartz and pebble finishes generally last longer than standard plaster. Water chemistry maintenance is the biggest factor in how long any finish survives.

Resurface or acid wash — how do I know which I need?

An acid wash removes surface staining from a finish that is still structurally sound. If the plaster is rough, etched, flaking, or worn through to gunite, an acid wash will only expose more of the problem — that's when resurfacing is the right call. We inspect first and tell you which one your pool actually needs.

Can you fix my pool light or bonding during the resurface?

Yes — that's one of the main reasons homeowners choose us for this job. We hold an electrical contractor license (EC13011994) alongside our pool license, so light fixtures, niches, and bonding can be inspected and corrected while the pool is drained, without bringing in a second contractor.

What finish options do you install?

Quartz, pebble, and polished aggregate finishes. We review samples with you and match the finish to your budget, the look you want, and how your household uses the pool.

Is your pricing upfront?

Yes. We provide upfront pricing on every resurfacing project before any work begins.
