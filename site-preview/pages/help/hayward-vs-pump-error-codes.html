Hayward Variable-Speed Pump Error Codes Explained

By Yanni Ramos, Licensed Pool, Electrical & Gas Contractor

Every fault message a Hayward TriStar VS, MaxFlo VS, Super Pump VS, or EcoStar drive can throw — Prime Failed, PFC-Hi, Drive Overheated, Motor Phase Lost — decoded, with the safe checks first. We're the Pinellas County company licensed for the electrical side of these repairs (CPC1459998 · EC13011994 · LI4527).

Unlike heaters, Hayward's VS drives mostly show plain-English messages on the display rather than two-letter codes. One exception: when a TriStar VS is wired to a Hayward automation control, some faults surface as numeric codes — 64 (DC voltage low) and 65 (DC voltage high) are covered below under DC voltage faults.

Jump to a fault
  • Prime Failed
  • Drive Is Overheated
  • PFC-Hi

Prime Failed — Couldn't Establish Prime in 15 Minutes

The pump ran its priming routine for 15 minutes without moving enough water. Usual causes: low water level, a loose pump lid or dried-out lid o-ring letting air in, a suction-side air leak, a closed suction valve, or a clogged skimmer line.

Try this first:

  • Top up the pool water level.
  • Empty the skimmer and pump baskets.
  • Reseat the pump lid and lube its o-ring with pool lube so it seals.
  • Open every suction-side valve.
  • Retry the prime.
If that doesn't clear it: pressure-testing the suction plumbing for leaks and replacing the shaft seal are pro repairs — stop here.

Drive Is Overheated / Heat Sink Overheat

The drive electronics exceeded their safe temperature. In Florida this is often exactly what it sounds like: debris packed into the cooling fins or fan, a too-tight enclosure (the drive wants roughly 12" of rear clearance), or direct Florida sun beating on the drive all afternoon.

Try this first:

  • Turn the pump off at the breaker first.
  • Clear leaves and debris from the fan and cooling fins.
  • Make sure the drive has ventilation clearance and, ideally, shade.
  • Restore power.
If that doesn't clear it: recurring heat-sink faults usually mean drive replacement, which is wiring work — an electrical repair. Stop here.

PFC-Hi — Incoming Voltage Spike (Not a Pump Defect)

Per Hayward's own technical service bulletin, PFC-Hi means the drive detected a spike in incoming AC voltage — above roughly 280 VAC — and latched itself off to protect its electronics. Read that again: the pump is fine; your power isn't. Around Tampa Bay, the lightning capital of the country, supply-side surges are a fact of life for pool equipment.

Try this first:

  • Reset the drive by cycling its breaker off and on.
  • If it recurs frequently, note when it happens (storms? evenings?) — that pattern points at supply-power quality.
If it keeps coming back: chasing recurring surges through panel connections and the utility supply is licensed electrical work — exactly our EC13011994 territory. Stop here.

DC Voltage Too High / Too Low (Codes 65 / 64 via Automation)

The incoming power is outside the drive's safe window — roughly 207–253 VAC on 230 V models (EcoStar also flags "AC Mains Low" below about 185 VAC). Causes are undersized wire for the run to the pad, loose lugs, or utility voltage problems. Through Hayward automation these show as numeric code 64 (low) or 65 (high).

Try this first:

  • Cycle the breaker once. That's the only safe check.
If that doesn't clear it: measuring voltage at the pump, upsizing conductors, and coordinating with the utility are licensed electrical work — stop here.

Drive Overload / Over Current

The motor is drawing more current than it should. The classic cause is a jammed impeller — debris or pebbles that got past the basket — but failing bearings, a leaking shaft seal, or a drive fault can do it too.

Try this first:

  • Power off at the breaker, then clean the pump basket.
  • Nothing further — do not open the wet-end housing.
If that doesn't clear it: impeller, diffuser, and seal inspection means opening the pump — a pro repair. Stop here.

Pump Has Stalled / Drive Failed to Start

The motor failed to spin up after its start attempts (typically three). That usually means a seized shaft, water intrusion into the drive, or loose motor leads — and it's usually a motor/drive replacement decision.

Try this first:

  • One breaker cycle. If it stalls again, stop and call.
If that doesn't clear it: everything else is inside the wired drive and motor — an electrical repair. Stop here.

Motor Phase Lost — Internal Winding Failure

One of the motor's winding phases is gone — water ingress, a failed winding, or a failed drive transistor. This is an internal hardware failure; power cycling won't fix it, and there are no homeowner steps.

Motor/drive assembly replacement is an electrical repair — stop here and call.

Memory Failure — Drive Control Memory Fault

The drive's control memory has failed (seen on EcoStar). Like any board-level fault, one power cycle rules out a glitch; if it survives that, the board is done.

Try this first:

  • One power cycle.
If that doesn't clear it: board-level work — a sealed-component repair. Stop here.

Check System — Generic Protective Stop

A catch-all: the drive stopped itself for voltage out of range, overcurrent, overheat, or a stall — often after several automatic restart attempts. Your job is to give the tech context.

Try this first:

  • Note the conditions — brutally hot afternoon? A storm just rolled through?
  • Cycle the breaker once and watch for a more specific message.
If it returns without a more specific code: drive diagnosis is electrical work — stop here.

Warning No Comm / Blank Display

The display and drive aren't communicating, or the display isn't getting power at all. The comm and display wiring lives inside the wired drive enclosure.

Try this first:

  • Confirm the breaker is on. Nothing else.
If that doesn't clear it: RS-485 and harness checks inside the drive enclosure are electrical work — stop here.

SVRS Tripped — Suction Safety Activated (SVRS Models)

The Safety Vacuum Release System detected a suction anomaly — a possible blockage or entrapment condition. SVRS is a life-safety system: treat a repeat trip as a real warning, not a nuisance.

Try this first:

  • Check and clean the skimmer and pump baskets and the filter.
  • Visually confirm all drain covers are intact and in place.
If it trips repeatedly, stop using the pump and call. Never bypass or disable an SVRS — this is a life-safety repair. Stop here.

Pump Keeps Tripping the Breaker

A pump that immediately re-trips its breaker has a wiring fault, a failing drive, or a GFCI compatibility/sensitivity problem — and every reset shoves fault current through the same damaged spot.

Try this first:

  • Do NOT keep resetting a breaker that immediately trips again. Leave it off and call.
Fault isolation — disconnecting motor leads to localize the short — is live electrical diagnosis. This is an electrical repair; stop here.
