Jandy AquaPure Error Codes 120–194 Explained

By Yanni Ramos, Licensed Pool, Electrical & Gas Contractor

This page decodes every Jandy AquaPure salt-system service code — the customer-visible 120-series codes and the tech-level 180-series behind them — plus TruClear panel messages, with the safe checks for each. Several of these codes are voltage and board faults: we're the Pinellas County company licensed for the electrical side of these repairs (EC13011994, CPC1459998).

Jump to a code
  • 120 / 121 — Low cell current
  • 123 — Extremely low current
  • 124 — High cell current
  • 125 — Cell needs cleaning
  • 126 / 127 — Low current + low voltage
  • 144 — Low salinity
  • 145 — High salinity
  • 170 — Front board / supply voltage
  • 171 — Back board
  • 172 — Flow sensor
  • 173 — Low input voltage
  • 174 — High water temperature
  • 175 — Sensor airlock / very low salt
  • Level 2 codes 180–195
  • TruClear messages

Quick orientation: AquaPure shows two layers of codes. Level 1 codes (120–175) appear on the display. Level 2 codes (180–195) are the underlying diagnostic that generates a Level 1 code — a technician reads those from the service menu. Salt readings assume a target of roughly 3.0–3.5 gpl.

Level 1 Service Codes (What You See on the Display)

120 / 121 — Low current to the cell

The system is pushing less current through the salt cell than it should — code 120 in the forward direction, 121 in reverse. The usual causes are a scaled or worn cell, or a poor DC cord connection at the cell.

Try this first:

  • Inspect the cell for white calcium scale and clean it per Jandy's acid-wash procedure.
  • Verify salt is around 3.0–3.5 gpl with independent test strips.
If that doesn't clear it: measuring the board's DC output is live electrical diagnosis — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

123 — Extremely low current to the cell

Same family as 120/121, but severe: the cell is heavily scaled, at end of life, or the DC cord has failed.

Try this first:

  • Clean the cell and check the cord ends for corrosion — unplug at the cell connector only.
If that doesn't clear it after a clean cell and cord check: back-board diagnosis — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

124 — Higher-than-normal current to the cell

Per the AquaPure code guide, code 124 indicates a back-board fault. There's no meaningful homeowner check for it.

This is an electrical repair — board fault. Stop here.

125 — Cell needs cleaning (low output)

Chlorine output has dropped below target. Usually a scaled cell; sometimes a tri-sensor issue or a cell at end of life (see code 194, the classic end-of-life signature).

Try this first:

  • Clean the cell and verify the salt level with test strips.
  • Power-cycle the system once.
If it recurs right after cleaning: cell replacement or sensor/board testing — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

126 / 127 — Low cell current plus low voltage

The cell current is low and the voltage feeding it is low — pointing at supply voltage, the transformer, or the back board rather than the cell (126 forward, 127 reverse).

Try this first:

  • Check the breaker feeding the system and confirm nothing else on the pad is tripping.
If that doesn't clear it: voltage and transformer testing is live electrical work — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

144 — Low salinity (below 2.0 gpl)

The system reads salt below 2.0 gpl. Real causes include not enough salt, dilution from heavy rain or splash-out — a regular event in a Pinellas summer — or cold water: below about 60 °F the reading runs artificially low.

Try this first:

  • Test the salt independently with strips before trusting the display.
  • If genuinely low, add pool salt toward 3.0–3.5 gpl — let it dissolve fully and retest before adding more.
If your test strips say the salt is fine but the code persists: sensor or board diagnosis — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

145 — High salinity (above 4.0 gpl)

The system reads salt above 4.0 gpl — either genuine over-salting or sensor drift.

Try this first:

  • Test independently with strips; if truly high, partially drain and refill to dilute.
If the reading is clearly wrong versus your test kit: tri-sensor replacement — this is a sealed-component repair. Stop here.

170 — Front board service condition / incorrect supply voltage

The front board reports a service condition, or the unit isn't receiving the correct 115 VAC — low AC voltage from the back board or a transformer issue.

Try this first:

  • Check the breaker feeding the system. That's the only safe step.
If that doesn't clear it: wiring, transformer and board work is licensed electrical repair — our specialty. Stop here.

171 — Back board service condition

The back board itself is faulting — commonly a defective board or an internal relay failure (Level 2 codes 189/190 underneath).

Try this first:

  • Power-cycle the system once.
If that doesn't clear it: board replacement — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

172 — Flow sensor not detecting flow

The flow sensor isn't seeing water movement — or the sensor itself has failed. Pump off, closed valves, a dirty sensor, or a loose sensor cable are the common causes.

Try this first:

  • Confirm the pump is running, valves are open, and the filter is clean.
  • Check that the sensor cable is plugged in — at the external connector only.
If that doesn't clear it: sensor replacement or board testing — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

173 — Low input voltage or wiring error

The unit sees supply voltage that's too low or wired to the wrong tap — a wrong voltage tap or loose supply wiring at install is the classic cause.

Try this first:

  • Check the breaker — nothing more.
If that doesn't clear it: supply-voltage verification is electrician work — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

174 — High water temperature (above 108 °F at the sensor)

The sensor reads water hotter than 108 °F — usually a spa-mode overshoot, occasionally a sensor error.

Try this first:

  • Let the water cool, and verify actual water temperature with a real thermometer.
If it keeps reading falsely high: tri-sensor replacement — this is a sealed-component repair. Stop here.

175 — Flow sensor airlock or very low salinity

Either air is trapped at the sensor or the salt level is near zero.

Try this first:

  • Run the pump to purge trapped air.
  • Verify salt with test strips.
If the airlock keeps coming back: plumbing or sensor-orientation correction — this is a pro repair. Stop here.

Level 2 Codes 180–195 (The Diagnostic Underneath)

Each Level 2 code is what actually generates the Level 1 code on your display — a technician reads them from the service menu. They're listed here so you know what your tech is talking about; every one of them is a professional repair (the only homeowner-relevant note: 182 means the system sees essentially zero salt, and 194 — cell current 85% below target with cell voltage above 19 V — is the classic signature of an end-of-life salt cell).

Jandy TruClear Panel Messages

No Flow — flow switch not detecting enough water

The TruClear's flow switch isn't seeing roughly 20 GPM or better.

Try this first:

  • Confirm the pump is on, baskets and filter are clean, and valves are open.
If that doesn't clear it: flow-switch cleaning or replacement — this is a pro repair. Stop here.

Check Cell — scaled or failing cell

The cell is scaled or on its way out.

Try this first:

  • Turn the breaker off, inspect and clean the cell, then restore power.
If it recurs: cell or board replacement — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.

Low salt — but your test strips disagree

When the panel says low salt and an independent test says otherwise, the real culprit is usually the flow switch or a scaled cell, not the salt. Note that genuinely cold water (below roughly 50 °F) also suppresses output legitimately — the system isn't broken on a cold January morning.

If cleaning the cell and the flow checks don't resolve it: sensor or board diagnosis — this is an electrical repair. Stop here.
