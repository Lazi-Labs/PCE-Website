What Size Whole House Generator Does Your Largo, FL Home Really Need?

By Yanni Ramos, Licensed Pool & Electrical Contractor (EC13011994)

The right generator size depends on your home’s total electrical load: the running wattage of everything you want powered, plus the surge wattage those appliances draw at startup. Most Largo homes need enough capacity to carry the air conditioner, refrigerator, lighting, and electronics through a multi-day outage, with room for future additions. A load calculation performed by a licensed electrician is the only reliable way to size it correctly, and Perfect Catch Electric performs that calculation before recommending any unit.

Why Install a Whole House Generator in Largo, FL?

A whole house generator keeps your home fully powered during an outage, automatically. It starts within seconds of the grid going down, carries your essential systems without interruption, and shuts itself off when utility power returns.

That automation matters most here during hurricane season, when a single storm moving across Pinellas County can knock out power for days. With a standby generator, food stays cold, air conditioning keeps running through the August heat, and you go about your life while the neighborhood waits on the utility.

There are quieter benefits too. A whole house generator protects your home’s electrical systems from the surges that come with outages and restoration, which extends the life of appliances and electronics. It also adds resale appeal, because buyers in coastal Florida understand exactly what a standby generator is worth.

Sizing and installing one correctly is electrical work from start to finish. As a licensed electrician in Largo, FL, we handle the load calculation, the transfer switch, the wiring, and the fuel connection under our own licenses, with no subcontractors on the job.

How Do You Calculate Your Generator Power Needs?

Generator sizing starts with a complete inventory of your home’s electrical load. That means identifying every appliance and system you want powered during an outage and totaling what they actually draw.

The essential list for most Largo homes includes the refrigerator, air conditioning and heating, lighting, well or pool equipment, and electronics. Each has a wattage rating, usually printed on a label or listed in the manual.

The number that trips up most homeowners is starting wattage. Motor-driven appliances like AC compressors and refrigerators draw a large surge of power at startup, well above their continuous running wattage. A generator sized only for running watts will stall the moment the air conditioner kicks on.

This is the core of what we do during a whole house generator installation assessment. We measure the real load, account for surge demand, and match the unit to the house instead of guessing from a brochure.

What Size Generator Fits Your Home?

The right size is the one that comfortably carries your total calculated load, surge included, with capacity to spare. Three numbers drive the decision:

  • Total wattage: The combined running watts of every essential appliance and system, from the refrigerator to the HVAC.
  • Surge power: The extra wattage motor-driven appliances demand at startup, which often exceeds their running draw by a wide margin.
  • Future needs: Any loads you plan to add later, such as a pool heater, a workshop, or an EV charger, so the generator does not become undersized in three years.

Your electrical panel is part of this equation too. An older panel may need attention before it can safely accept a transfer switch and the full generator load. We evaluate the panel during sizing, and when a home needs an electrical panel upgrade, we handle it in the same scope rather than splitting the job across contractors.

Key Factors for Whole House Generator Sizing

Beyond wattage math, four factors shape the final recommendation: your home’s total electrical load, your future plans, the fuel type, and local code requirements.

The load assessment comes first. Every essential appliance and system gets listed with its wattage requirement, which establishes the real power demand during an outage. Homes in Largo’s older neighborhoods off West Bay Drive often carry different load profiles than newer construction near Ulmerton Road, so we size from measurements, not assumptions.

Future plans deserve honest consideration. If a home addition, a pool, or new equipment is on the horizon, building in extra capacity now costs far less than replacing an undersized generator later.

Fuel type affects both performance and practicality. Natural gas and propane generators differ in efficiency, availability, and installation requirements. Perfect Catch Electric holds LP gas license LI4527, so the fuel connection is handled by us, legally and correctly, not handed off to a third party.

Finally, permits and code compliance in Largo, FL, and Pinellas County are part of every install. Our residential electrical services team pulls the permits and executes the work under our own electrical license, EC13011994, from first inspection to final sign-off.

How Do You Keep a Generator Ready for the Next Outage?

A standby generator is only as reliable as its maintenance. Three habits keep it ready:

  • Schedule routine inspections: Annual check-ups by a licensed technician catch small issues before they become failures during a storm.
  • Change the oil: Like a car engine, a generator needs oil changes at the intervals the manufacturer specifies, especially after extended run time.
  • Test it monthly: A monthly exercise run confirms the unit starts, transfers, and carries load, and surfaces problems while there is still time to fix them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size generator does it take to run a whole house in Florida?

Whole house standby generators for Florida homes commonly fall in the range of roughly 18 to 26 kilowatts, but the correct size depends entirely on your home’s measured electrical load. Square footage alone does not determine it. Air conditioning demand, appliance count, and surge loads drive the real number, which is why a load calculation comes before any recommendation.

Will a whole house generator run my air conditioner?

Yes, if it is sized for the AC’s starting surge, which is usually the largest single load in a Florida home. A compressor can demand several times its running wattage at startup. This is the most common sizing mistake we correct, and it is the main reason undersized generators stall during summer outages.

What fuel do whole house generators use in Largo, FL?

Most standby generators in Largo, FL, run on natural gas or propane. Natural gas offers an uninterrupted supply where service is available, while propane stores on-site and works anywhere. Because we hold both electrical and LP gas licenses, we can install and connect either configuration without bringing in a second contractor.

How fast does a standby generator turn on when the power goes out?

A whole house standby generator starts and transfers your home’s load automatically, typically within about 10 to 30 seconds of an outage. There are no cords to run and no switches to flip, which matters when a storm hits overnight or while you are away from home. The automatic transfer switch handles the changeover in both directions.
