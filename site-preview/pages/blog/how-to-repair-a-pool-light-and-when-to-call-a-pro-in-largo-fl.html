How to Repair a Pool Light
(And When to Call a Pro in Largo, FL)

By Yanni Ramos, Licensed Pool & Electrical Contractor (EC13011994)

A beautifully lit swimming pool is the centerpiece of any Florida backyard. It transforms your space into a resort-like oasis for evening swims and entertaining. But when that brilliant light starts to flicker, dim, or goes out completely, it does more than ruin the mood—it creates a genuine safety hazard.

While the temptation to tackle a pool light repair yourself is understandable, it's crucial to recognize that this isn't like changing a lightbulb in your living room. Mixing water and electricity requires extreme caution and expertise.

This guide is designed to walk you through the basic steps of diagnosing your pool light problem and handling a simple bulb change safely. More importantly, it will help you identify the critical warning signs that mean it's time to call a professional for pool light service in Largo, FL. At Perfect Catch Swimming Pool Repair, Gas, & Electric, we believe an informed homeowner is a safe homeowner.

Safety First: The Critical Rules of Pool Electrical Work

Before you even think about touching your pool light, you must internalize these non-negotiable safety rules. Electricity and water are a lethal combination, and there is no room for error.

  • Rule #1: Shut Off ALL Power. This means going to your home's main circuit breaker panel and flipping the specific breaker for your pool equipment (light, pump, heater) to the "OFF" position. Don't just rely on the switch by the equipment.
  • Rule #2: Verify the Power is Off. Use a voltage tester at the light switch to confirm that there is no active current. Never assume the breaker is labeled correctly.
  • Rule #3: Understand Your GFCI. Your pool light is (or should be) protected by a Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI). This device is designed to shut off the power instantly if it detects a fault, like water getting into the electrical system. If your GFCI is tripping, it's doing its job and warning you of a serious problem.

Diagnosing the Problem: Why Is Your Pool Light Out?

Figuring out the root cause can help you determine if you have a simple DIY fix on your hands or a more complex issue requiring a professional.

Is it Just a Burnt-Out Bulb?

This is the best-case scenario. Like any bulb, pool light bulbs have a finite lifespan and will eventually burn out. If the light simply went dark one day without any flickering or GFCI trips, the bulb itself is the most likely culprit.

Is the GFCI Tripped?

Go to your GFCI outlet (it's often a specific outlet near your pool equipment with "TEST" and "RESET" buttons). If it's tripped, press the "RESET" button firmly. If the light comes back on and stays on, you may have experienced a momentary, harmless fault.

However, if the GFCI trips again immediately or frequently, stop what you are doing. This is a clear sign that water is likely leaking into the fixture or there's a fault in the wiring. This is no longer a DIY job.

Is There Water in the Light Housing?

Look closely at the light's lens. Do you see condensation, moisture, or a small puddle of water inside the fixture? This is a major red flag. A compromised seal has allowed water to penetrate the housing, creating a direct path for electricity to enter your pool water. This requires immediate professional attention.

How to Repair a Pool Light: A Step-by-Step DIY Guide (For Simple Fixes)

Disclaimer: This guide is for replacing a standard incandescent bulb in an older, non-sealed fixture. Modern LED lights are typically fully sealed units that require the entire fixture to be replaced by a professional. If you are unsure or uncomfortable at any point, call a pro.
  1. Turn Off All Power: We can't say it enough. Go to your breaker box and shut down all power to the pool equipment. Verify with a voltage tester.
  2. Remove the Fixture from the Pool Wall: Most pool lights are held in place by a single pilot screw at the top of the fixture. Using a Phillips head screwdriver, remove this screw. The fixture should then tilt forward. Pool lights are designed with a very long cord (15-100 feet) coiled up behind them, allowing you to pull the entire housing out of the water and place it on your pool deck to work on it.
  3. Open the Housing and Replace the Bulb: With the fixture on the deck, carefully dry the entire housing. Loosen the screws holding the faceplate and lens in place. Gently pry the lens off and inspect the rubber gasket seal. This gasket is the most critical component. If it is dry, cracked, or stretched, it MUST be replaced. A faulty gasket is the #1 reason lights fail. Unscrew the old bulb and screw in the new one, ensuring it's the correct voltage and wattage for your system.
  4. Reassemble and Test: Install your new gasket, ensuring it's seated perfectly. Re-attach the lens and faceplate, tightening the screws in a star pattern to ensure even pressure. Before putting the light back in the wall, briefly turn the power on to confirm the new bulb works. Power it off again, and then carefully feed the cord back into the niche as you guide the fixture back into place. Secure it with the pilot screw.

When DIY Becomes Dangerous: Signs You MUST Call a Professional

Knowing your limits is key to your safety. Call a licensed pool electrician in Largo immediately if you encounter any of the following:

  • Water Inside the Light: This is an urgent safety issue.
  • A Constantly Tripping GFCI: This indicates a dangerous ground fault.
  • A Brittle or Cracked Cord: The protective sheath on the cord is compromised.
  • You Have a Sealed LED Unit: These cannot be repaired and require a full fixture replacement.
  • The Problem Isn't the Bulb: If a new bulb doesn't fix it, the issue lies deeper in the system (transformer, junction box, or wiring).
  • You're Not 100% Confident: There is no shame in calling for help. Your family's safety is worth more than a DIY project.
