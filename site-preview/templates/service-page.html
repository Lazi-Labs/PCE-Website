How to Tell Your Pool Pump Needs Repair

Pool pumps signal problems before they fail completely. Catching the issue at the warning stage usually keeps a repair viable and avoids a full motor replacement.

  • Grinding, squealing, or rattling sounds during operation
  • Pump runs but water flow is noticeably reduced
  • Pump loses prime frequently or fails to prime at all
  • Motor overheating or shutting off mid-cycle
  • Visible cracks or leaks around the housing or connections
  • Energy bills climbing without a clear explanation

Common Pool Pump Problems We Fix

Florida's heat and year-round operating schedule accelerate wear on seals, bearings, capacitors, and impellers faster than seasonal climates. We repair failed capacitors, worn shaft seals, clogged impellers, overheating motors, and corroded housings across all major brands.

Because our technicians hold electrical licenses in addition to pool credentials, we catch electrical faults behind pump failures that a pool-only contractor would miss.

Choosing the Right Pump for Your Pool

Single-speed pumps are simple but expensive to run year-round. Variable-speed pumps use significantly less electricity, often qualify for utility rebates, and are our standard recommendation for Largo's twelve-month season. We walk you through the options — no oversell, no pressure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do pool pumps last?

Most pool pumps last eight to twelve years, though Florida's year-round operation can shorten that range. Regular maintenance and prompt attention to warning signs extend pump life considerably.

Should I repair or replace my pump?

A licensed technician evaluates the pump's age, condition, and repair cost versus replacement cost. We give honest guidance based on what actually makes financial sense for your situation.
